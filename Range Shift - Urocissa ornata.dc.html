A range that barely moves, and the effort that only looks like growth

Thirty-four years of Urocissa ornata occurrence records, binned into seven five-year windows and smoothed into a kernel density surface over a fixed inland window of Sri Lanka's wet zone. The density-weighted centre of the surface drifts a handful of kilometres. The number of records behind it grows ninety-fold. Only one of those two facts is about birds.

Caveat — sampling effort

The 1990–94 surface rests on 22 records; 2020–24 on 2,009. That ninety-fold jump tracks the arrival of structured citizen science, not ninety times more magpies. Read the later surfaces as better-observed, not more populated.

Method — bandwidth

A single pooled bandwidth of 0.8972 km (rounded to 0.90) was cross-validated on all bins together and then held fixed, so the seven surfaces stay comparable. Per-bin bandwidths would have over-smoothed the thin early windows into near-flatness.

Each surface is normalised to its own maximum before subtraction, so this map answers where the probability mass moved within a bin — not whether there were more birds. Rust is gain of relative weight, green is loss. Against the 22-record 1990–94 baseline, almost every panel shows large, confident-looking structure; almost none of it is trustworthy. The comparisons that carry weight are the late-to-late ones.

Read this map twice

Once for the pattern, once for the sample sizes behind it. A difference between n = 22 and n = 29 is a difference between two sketches. A difference between n = 1,009 and n = 2,009 is a difference between two surveys.

§ 05

Method, and what this figure is not

Estimator

Isotropic gaussian kernel density on a 300 × 300 lattice spanning 6.116–7.455 °N, 80.081–80.981 °E. Cells are 0.49 km north–south and 0.33 km east–west; the display preserves that anisotropy rather than squaring the grid.

Bandwidth

h = 0.8972 km, selected by cross-validation on the pooled record set and held constant across all seven bins. Stated, not tuned per panel. At this bandwidth a single isolated record produces a visible ~2 km bloom — worth remembering when looking at 1990–94.

Effort bias

Records are opportunistic, not survey-designed. Density here is density of observations. No effort correction, no detection model, no accessibility covariate has been applied — roads, reserve boundaries and birder itineraries are all in this surface.

Not a range shift

Nothing here supports a claim about range expansion, contraction or climate-driven movement. Frames cross-dissolve between bins for legibility; no values are interpolated — every frame is a separately computed surface. The honest headline is a stable core around Sinharaja and a record set that grew up around it.
